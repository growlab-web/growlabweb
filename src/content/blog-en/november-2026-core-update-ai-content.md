On **November 10, 2026**, Google started rolling out its **November core update**, a broad change to its ranking systems that applies globally and to all languages. It lands right before Black Friday and Cyber Monday, so it is normal to see your rankings and organic traffic move these days.

At the same time, many brands are publishing content with the help of AI. In October, Google updated its guidance on AI-generated content, and the two are worth reading together: what to do during the update, and how to use AI without putting your rankings at risk.

## What a core update is and what we know about this one

According to Google’s documentation, core updates are **broad, significant changes** to its search systems, made several times a year. They **don’t target specific sites or pages**: the goal is to show helpful, reliable results, and most sites don’t need to do anything.

What is confirmed about the November update:

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Start | November 10, 2026, at 10:06 a.m. Pacific Time, per the Search Status Dashboard |
| Scope | Global, all languages |
| Duration | Google said the rollout may take **up to 2 weeks** |
| Status | At the time of writing, Google had not yet marked it as complete |

It is the third core update of the year, after the March and May 2026 updates. Google also ran a **spam update** in September that finished in early October.

## How to tell if it affected you (without jumping to conclusions)

While the rollout is in progress, rankings go up and down. Google recommends not reacting halfway through and following these steps:

1. **Wait for the rollout to finish.** Google announces it on its Search Status Dashboard.
2. **Let at least one full week pass** after it ends.
3. In Search Console, **compare that week with the week before November 10**.
4. Review your **top pages and queries**. Dropping from position 2 to 4 doesn’t call for drastic changes; a large, sustained drop deserves a deeper look.
5. Check **Web, Images, Video and News** separately to see where the drop happened.

If you sell online, cross-check with GA4 too: a dip in organic traffic in the middle of the sales season may come from seasonality or your campaigns, not just the algorithm. If you also want to separate traffic coming from AI assistants, see our guide on [ChatGPT and other AI traffic in GA4](/en/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic-ga4).

## What to do if your site dropped

Google doesn’t offer a recipe for “recovering”, but its documentation gives some clear pointers:

- **Avoid quick fixes**, such as removing page elements only because you think they hurt SEO.
- **Look at your site as a whole**, as objectively as you can; you can even ask someone outside your company for their view.
- **Genuinely improve your content**: rewrite, restructure and update the most affected pages with the reader in mind.
- **Deleting content is a last resort**, although Google notes that if whole sections exist only for search engines, removing them may help the rest of the site.
- **Don’t touch what is working well.**

How long until you see improvements? Some show up within days, but confirming a lasting improvement can take **several months**, and sometimes you need to wait for the next core update. No change guarantees getting your previous rankings back.

## What Google says about AI-generated content

On **October 1, 2026**, Google updated its guidance on using generative AI content, adding information from the guidelines for its Search Quality Raters. The key points:

- **Using AI is not forbidden.** It can help you research or add structure to original content.
- **Mass production is what counts as spam.** The *scaled content abuse* policy includes using generative AI to create many pages **without adding value** for users.
- **Fact-check by hand.** Google reminds you that AI models don’t retrieve facts but predict text, and asks you to manually review everything generated before publishing.
- **The review includes metadata**: titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text, because they can appear in search results.
- **Give readers context.** Explaining how a piece of content was created (for example, which part was automated) can be useful.
- **If you sell through Google Merchant Center**, AI-generated images must carry the IPTC metadata that says so, and AI-generated product titles and descriptions must be labeled as such.

The rater guidelines are not a guide to ranking first on Google and don’t directly influence rankings, but they show what Google considers quality content.

## Checklist for your content (with or without AI)

These questions come from Google’s guidance on helpful content:

1. Does it offer **original information, analysis or experience**, or does it just repeat what already exists?
2. Is it written or reviewed by **someone who knows the topic well**?
3. Does it contain **easily verified factual errors**?
4. Does it look **carefully produced**, or rushed?
5. Is it something a customer would **bookmark or share**?
6. Does the reader leave **with their question answered**?

If you use AI to write product pages or blog posts, the practical rule is simple: let AI do the first draft, and have a person add the real data, examples from your business and the final review. It is the same advice we give for showing up in [Google’s AI Mode](/en/blog/google-ai-mode-peru-seo).

## In short

The November 2026 core update is under way and may take up to two weeks. Don’t make changes blindly: wait for it to finish, compare your Search Console data and, if you dropped, improve your content for real. And if you use AI, use it to work better, not to publish more pages without value: check every fact and every piece of metadata before publishing.
