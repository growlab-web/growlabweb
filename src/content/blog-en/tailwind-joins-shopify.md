On September 9, 2026, Adam Wathan, creator of Tailwind CSS, announced that **Tailwind Labs is joining Shopify**. It is big news for web development: Tailwind is one of the most widely used tools for building interfaces and, according to the company itself, **it is installed more than 110 million times a week**. This very GrowLab website is built with Tailwind CSS.

## What was announced

In its announcement, Tailwind Labs explains that it is joining Shopify to give the framework **“a stable long-term home”** where it will keep being maintained for the millions of people who depend on it. The financial terms of the deal were not disclosed; several outlets describe it as an acquisition.

Shopify is no newcomer: according to the announcement, it was **one of the first companies to use Tailwind at scale** and today considers it an important part of its own platform.

## What happens to Tailwind CSS

For those who use it, the essentials **do not change**:

- **It remains open source and MIT-licensed**, like the rest of Tailwind Labs’ open-source projects.
- **The same team** will keep leading and maintaining the framework, now with Shopify’s support.

If your website or store uses Tailwind, you do not need to migrate anything or switch tools.

## What does change: the paid products

The change is on the commercial side. Tailwind Labs will no longer try to grow the business around Tailwind:

| Product | Existing customers | New customers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tailwind CSS (free, MIT) | No change | No change |
| Tailwind Plus (paid components and templates) | Keep their access | Sign-ups closed |
| ui.sh | Keep their access | Sign-ups closed |

If your team was planning to buy Tailwind Plus, signing up is no longer possible. Any components you already have remain yours.

## What it means for online sellers

1. **More stability for a key tool.** Having a company the size of Shopify behind it reduces the risk of the framework being abandoned or half-maintained.
2. **A signal about where Shopify is heading.** The announcement mentions that Shopify is at the frontier of new interfaces with its explorations into **agentic commerce**, that is, AI-assisted shopping. It is reasonable to expect Tailwind to become more integrated into Shopify’s tools for building custom storefronts.
3. **Nothing urgent to do.** If your store runs on a Shopify theme, this news does not change your day-to-day. If you work with a custom (headless) store, Tailwind remains a safe bet.

## In summary

Tailwind CSS is still free, open and maintained by the same team, now with Shopify behind it. The only thing closing is the sale of its paid products to new customers. For brands and online stores it is good news: one of the most widely used technical foundations of the web now has long-term backing.
