For many businesses in Latin America, WhatsApp is the most important sales channel: it is where campaign leads arrive, orders are closed and customers are served. That is why a banned number can stop sales overnight. The good news is that it almost always has a clear cause and can be prevented.

## What WhatsApp says about bans

According to the WhatsApp Help Center, accounts are banned when their activity violates its Terms of Service, for example through **spam, fraud or activity that puts users’ safety at risk**. When it happens, the app shows the message *“This phone number is banned from using WhatsApp.”*

In its business policy, WhatsApp makes it clear that its priority is **high-quality conversations** between people and businesses, and that it may **limit or suspend the access** of a business that breaks its rules. In serious cases, it may **remove the account and prevent the business from using its products again**.

## The most common causes

### 1. Messaging people who did not give permission

The WhatsApp Business Policy requires the **person’s permission (opt-in)** before sending them messages. Messaging numbers from bought lists, scraped from the internet or from people who gave you their number for something else is the fastest route to a restriction.

### 2. Ignoring people who ask to stop receiving messages

WhatsApp states that you must **honor all opt-out requests**, inside and outside the app, and remove that person from your list. Continuing to message someone after “please stop messaging me” is a direct violation.

### 3. Many blocks and reports from users

People can block or report a business, and WhatsApp uses that feedback to **limit how many messages** that number can send. Irrelevant or overly frequent messages, or messages to people who did not expect them, lower the number’s quality.

### 4. Unofficial apps and tools

Modified versions of WhatsApp and programs that automate bulk sending from the regular app or WhatsApp Web break the terms. The policy warns that if you use a service that operates WhatsApp in a way that violates its rules, they may **limit or remove your access**.

### 5. A recycled number

If your phone number is new, WhatsApp clarifies that **the previous owner may have violated the terms**, which can also cause a ban.

## Messaging limits on the official API

If your business uses the **WhatsApp Business API** (the official way to send messages at scale, through Meta or an authorized provider), there is a limit on how many **unique people** you can reach with templates in a rolling 24-hour period, outside conversations the customer starts:

| Tier | Unique people every 24 hours | How to reach it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting | 250 | All new businesses start here |
| 2 | 2,000 | By verifying your business or sending 2,000 high-quality messages within 30 days |
| 3 to 5 | 10,000, 100,000 and unlimited | Automatically, if quality stays high and you use at least half your limit |

Meta calculates these limits **per business portfolio**, not per number: all your numbers share the same capacity. The key to moving up is the **quality rating** of your messages.

## What about AI chatbots?

Since **January 15, 2026**, Meta’s new terms **no longer allow general-purpose AI assistants** on the WhatsApp Business API (services such as ChatGPT or Perplexity stopped working there). Meta clarified that the change **does not affect businesses that use AI to serve their customers**: bots for support, sales, FAQs, order tracking or appointment booking are still allowed.

## What to do if your number is banned

1. In the app, tap **“Request a review.”** WhatsApp says reviews are usually resolved within **24 hours**, and it will notify you of its decision.
2. If the review option does not appear, the ban is **permanent** and cannot be appealed.
3. Be wary of anyone offering to “unban” your number: according to WhatsApp, **no third-party service can ban or unban accounts**, and contacting it through other channels does not speed up the process.
4. While the ban lasts you cannot access your chat history or backups, so it is wise to keep your key contacts and conversations in a CRM as well.

## Checklist to keep your number safe

1. Use the **official API** (directly or through an authorized provider) if you message at scale, never modified apps or bulk-sending programs.
2. Ask for **explicit permission** before messaging, ideally by message type (orders, promotions, reminders).
3. Offer a clear way to **opt out** and honor it immediately.
4. **Verify your business** in Meta Business Manager.
5. Send **relevant, well-spaced** messages: fewer, better-targeted sends protect your quality.
6. Check the **quality rating** of your number and templates every week.
7. Use click-to-WhatsApp ads so that **the customer starts** the conversation.

## In summary

WhatsApp does not ban accounts at random: it does so when it detects spam, lack of consent or unauthorized tools. Working with the official API, with contacts who gave their permission and taking care of the quality of every message protects your number and, with it, one of your most valuable sales channels.
