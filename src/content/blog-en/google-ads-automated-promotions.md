Google Ads has a new way to show offers in your ads: **automated promotions**. Instead of you creating a promotion asset with the discount and dates, Google reads your website’s landing pages, detects active offers (for example, “20% off” or “buy one, get one free”) and adds them to your ads on its own.

For a store that keeps its website up to date, this can help during the peak sales season. But if an old banner or a poorly explained promotion is still on your site, it can also end up in your ads. That is why it pays to know exactly when it turns on and how to control it.

## What automated promotions are

According to the Google Ads Help Center, automated promotions are an AI-powered feature that **identifies, validates and displays offers from your website** in your ads, with no manual work. Google says it checks that an offer is active and not expired before showing it.

They are part of **account-level automated assets**, the same group of assets Google creates on its own (such as some dynamic sitelinks or callouts). In other words, they are not set up campaign by campaign but for the whole account.

Google published the help page in early September 2026, and in October it emailed some advertisers saying the feature would be **turned on by default on October 12, 2026**, as reported by Search Engine Roundtable. In the settings, the option appears as “On (recommended),” so if nobody changed it, it is probably already on in your account.

## Which campaigns can show them

Not every campaign qualifies. According to Google, a campaign must meet **all** of these conditions:

| Requirement | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign type | Search or Performance Max |
| Status | Active campaign |
| Location assets | Must have linked location assets (including Local Ad campaigns) |
| Manual promotions | Must not have manual promotions running: if it does, Google skips it |
| Policies | Ads must comply with Google Ads policies |

Two important details:

- **If your campaign has no location assets** (linked, for example, to your Google Business Profile), it will not receive automated promotions.
- **Promotions you create take priority**: a campaign with active manual promotions is left out of the automated system.

## How long they run and where they show

Google explains that each automated promotion serves for **less than 24 hours**. It can then be re-enabled if Google keeps verifying that the offer is still visible on your website. The help page itself notes that this will change once the system can extract promotion start and end dates.

As a promotion asset, it shows like any other: below text ads on Google Search, among other formats. Adding promotion assets is free; you pay for clicks, at the same cost as a click on the ad’s headline.

## How to check whether they already appear in your account

1. In Google Ads, go to **Campaigns → Assets**.
2. Filter by **Asset type → Promotion**.
3. Look at the **“Added by”** column: automated promotions are labeled **“Google AI.”**
4. To measure results, segment your campaign reports by **click type**. Google also recommends evaluating the whole campaign’s impressions and conversions, not just clicks on the promotion.

If you already use Google’s AI agent to review your accounts, you can ask it for this analysis directly (we explain it in [Ask Advisor: Google’s AI agent for Ads and Analytics](/en/blog/ask-advisor-google-ads-analytics-guide)).

## How to turn them off (or keep them under control)

If you prefer to decide which offers appear in your ads, the path Google gives is:

1. **Campaigns → Assets**.
2. In the filter bar, click the three-dot icon and choose **Account-level automated assets**.
3. On that page, open the three-dot menu and go to **Advanced settings**.
4. Under **Automated promotions**, choose **Off**.

Keep in mind that this setting applies **to the whole account**. If you want control over only some campaigns, the alternative is to create **manual promotions** in them: you set the text, discount, code and dates yourself, and those campaigns are left out of the automated system.

## Checklist before leaving them on

1. **Clean up your website**: remove banners for expired offers and promotional copy that no longer applies. Google explicitly asks you to keep your site’s promotional details up to date.
2. **Explain the conditions clearly** on the page (minimum spend, limited stock, in-store or online only) to avoid complaints.
3. **Check the “Added by” column every week** during big sales periods such as Black Friday, Cyber Monday or the holidays.
4. **Create manual promotions** for your key offers: they take priority and you control the dates.
5. **Compare results** with and without promotions before deciding to turn them off for the whole account.

## In summary

Automated promotions turn the offers on your website into assets for your Search and Performance Max ads, as long as the campaign has location assets and no manual promotions. They can help in peak season, but only if your website is up to date. Check today whether they already appear in your account, and decide whether to keep them on, turn them off or take control with manual promotions.
